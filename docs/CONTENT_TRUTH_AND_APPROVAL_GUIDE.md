# Content Truth and Approval Guide

## Principle

The website distinguishes a useful structure from a published claim. A route or component may exist before its editorial content is approved; it must then show an accurate empty or unavailable state instead of fabricated content.

## Content states

| State | Meaning | Allowed presentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `verified` | Evidence and publication approval are available | Publish with accurate source context |
| `editable` | Team-owned explanatory copy that can be refined | Publish as non-factual descriptive copy |
| `future` | Intended direction, not current operation | Clearly identify as future context |
| `pending` | Not approved or incomplete | Hide or show an explicit empty state |

Availability badges serve a separate purpose: they explain whether a service or faculty support path is available, limited, digital, or coming later.

## Evidence required before publication

| Content type | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- |
| News or event | Approved headline, date, body copy, owner, and publication authorization |
| Gallery item | Asset rights, contextual caption, alt text, and approval |
| Statistic / impact claim | Source, measurement period, owner, and approved wording |
| Partner / sponsor | Confirmed relationship wording and logo-use permission |
| Testimonial | Consent, approved attribution, and context |
| Contact channel | Exact destination, label, owner confirmation |
| Faculty-specific contact | Faculty-owner approval and central configuration entry |
| Download / platform access | Public release approval, destination, and support ownership |

## Editorial prohibitions

- Do not invent dates, counts, attendance, outcomes, sponsors, rankings, or institutional endorsements.
- Do not publish student images or personal details without rights and context approval.
- Do not imply active data collection, volunteer acceptance, a public platform release, or customer support where none exists.
- Do not turn a future vision into a present-tense operational promise.

## Approval workflow

1. Content owner supplies source material and permissions.
2. Editorial reviewer validates wording, locale, audience, and accessibility text.
3. Brand reviewer validates logo and visual-identity usage.
4. Authorized owner approves publication.
5. Update the typed content/configuration source, then perform Phase 3 runtime checks.
6. Maintain an external approval record with owner and date; do not encode private approvals in public code.
